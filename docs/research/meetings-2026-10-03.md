# Meetings: what it would take for aivi to sit in one

Research 2026-10-03, web sources as of this date. The question: can aivi
join a Google Meet (and Teams, and Zoom) as a participant — listen, speak
with a persistent voice, maybe show a face — and what do we build.
Conclusion first: **yes, and none of it is Google's.** The platforms
divide into two kinds, and the seams in aivi that already exist (channel
modules, the persistent Chrome, the jobs system) are where the work
attaches.

## The landscape, platform by platform

**Google Meet — no API at all.** There is no official bot, media, or
transcript API. Every product that "integrates with Meet" (Recall.ai,
Meeting BaaS, Nylas Notetaker, MeetStream) drives a real browser that
joins through the meeting link as a guest. That is browser automation
over the web client, and it is the only path.

**Microsoft Teams — an API exists, but not for us.** The official real-time
media path is the Real-time Media Platform via
`Microsoft.Graph.Communications.Calls.Media`: a *real* socket-like API,
frame-level audio and video, bot joins meetings and sends media. But it
is .NET on Windows, full stop — Microsoft Q&A answers from 2025/2026
confirm there is no Node.js, Python, REST or WebSocket interface and no
announced plan. Post-meeting transcripts and recordings come through
Graph in any language. So Teams has three tiers: Graph after the meeting
(easy, any stack), browser-join during it (what third-party notetakers
do), and a .NET media bot (the only sanctioned live-audio path, wrong
stack — a later "if ever" adapter).

**Zoom — the friendly one.** Zoom shipped **RTMS (Real-Time Media
Streams)** with an official **Node.js SDK**: live per-participant audio,
video, transcripts, screen share and participant events, delivered to
your app over webhooks — no browser, no scraping. (`rtms-quickstart-js`
is the quickstart; decode via native bindings, callback-driven.) Since
2026-03-02 cross-account meeting joins need an authorized app (ZAK/OBF
tokens or RTMS). Receiving is fully Node. *Speaking* needs the native
Meeting SDK (Windows/macOS/Linux C++) which supports **raw audio send**
(inject PCM as the bot's microphone) and raw video send — a small native
sidecar if we want aivi's voice in Zoom, or the same browser trick as
Meet for now. Note 2026-03+ also requires bots joining outside their
account to be authorized — a consent gate Zoom enforces platform-side.

**The pattern across all three:** *listening* is solved twice officially
(Zoom RTMS; Teams post-meeting) and once by browser (Meet, Teams live);
*speaking* is official only in Zoom's native SDK, and everywhere else is
the browser mic trick.

## The browser path (Meet, and Teams live) — how the bots do it

This is a solved engineering problem with open references:

1. **Join.** Launch or *attach to* Chrome with the media flags
   (`--use-fake-ui-for-media-stream`, `--use-fake-device-for-media-stream`),
   open the meet.google.com link, click join, stay muted by default.
   Playwright is the usual driver; it can `connectOverCDP` to an
   **already-running Chrome with a signed-in profile** — which is exactly
   what `aivi_browser` already keeps (persistent Chrome, unattended,
   shared logins). The open-source `screenappai/meeting-bot` (MIT,
   TypeScript/Playwright, joins Meet + Teams + Zoom) explicitly supports
   attaching to an existing Chrome via a CDP URL with a Google profile.
2. **Listen.** Two ways, both proven: scrape the **captions DOM** (Meet's
   own live captions, speaker-labelled; the Recall.ai and Gladia
   references), or capture the WebRTC **audio tracks** in-page and run
   our own STT (more control, our own diarization). Captions are the
   cheap start; raw audio is the quality end.
3. **Speak.** The mic-injection pattern, in production in several repos:
   the meeting page records from a **fake/virtual microphone**, and a
   pump streams TTS PCM into that virtual device — macOS **BlackHole**,
   Linux Pulse null-sink. `NousResearch/hermes-agent`'s `google_meet`
   plugin ships exactly this (Playwright join, caption scrape in,
   Realtime TTS PCM out through BlackHole/null-sink, auto-unmute after
   admission), and its issue tracker documents the real failure modes
   (joined-but-muted, the pump exiting early) — free debugging. The
   cleaner in-page variant skips OS devices: override
   `navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia` to return a stream fed from an
   `AudioContext` destination node. Barge-in (stop talking when a human
   speaks) exists in the wild too (`CoderOMaster/meeting-assistant`:
   mic control, echo detection, stops mid-sentence).
4. **Show a face.** Same mechanism one level up: draw aivi's avatar on a
   `<canvas>` in-page, `canvas.captureStream()` as the video track
   behind the same `getUserMedia` override. No virtual webcam needed in
   the browser path; Zoom's native SDK takes raw video frames directly.

The honest risks: Meet does bot-detection (headless signatures;
mitigated by attaching to a real signed-in Chrome, which is our shape
anyway — but it is a cat-and-mouse tax), the DOM scrape breaks when Meet
redesigns, and browser automation sits in a gray zone of the ToS. Zoom's
RTMS is the fully sanctioned door; Meet's is not sanctioned at all.

## Voice — ears and mouth, local on the Mac

The host is Apple Silicon with MLX already serving models, so the audio
stack has an all-local shape (BloopML `mlx-audio` / `mlx-speech` /
`gafiatulin+vibevoice-mlx`):

- **Mouth: VibeVoice.** MIT weights (1.5B, 7B, and a **Realtime-0.5B**
  streaming variant). The persistence mechanism the operator found is
  real and first-class: encode a reference clip once into a voice
  embedding (`--save-voice` → `.safetensors`) and synthesize with
  `--voice` thereafter — bit-identical, and the fork even caches the
  per-voice prefix KV (~100 ms prefill, ~3 s snapshot boot). Voice
  identity files would live in the home next to `.env`, 0600 discipline.
  Realtime-0.5B is the low-latency choice for conversation; 1.5B/7B for
  richer voice. Kokoro-82M is the instant-fallback voice.
- **Ears: local STT, streaming.** `mlx-whisper` (and whisper-streaming
  patterns), NVIDIA **Parakeet** (fast, on-device), or **VibeVoice-ASR**
  (9B, diarization + timestamps — speaker labels for free, which is what
  a meeting needs). A VAD (silero, ONNX) segments speech between STT
  calls. Cloud STT (Deepgram/Gladia) is the drop-in alternative; the
  self-hosted story does not need it.
- **Plumbing.** MLX is Python; aivi is Node. A sidecar child process
  with framed stdio (the EmZod fork ships exactly such a worker, built
  for a resident "audio-now" daemon) — same shape as the CLI exec relay:
  PCM bytes and small JSON in, PCM out. The host never loads a model.

## The generic roles — not Google, not calendar-specific

Same move as forge/tracker: **core spells the roles, adapters fill them,
nothing upstream knows which suite answers.**

| Role | What it answers | Adapters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **calendar** | *when and where* — upcoming events, join URLs, free/busy | Google Calendar API (push watch channels), MS Graph (`onlineMeetings`, webhook subscriptions), **CalDAV via `tsdav`** (the suite-agnostic floor: works with Fastmail/Apple/Nextcloud/Google; ctag polling only — shaped as a system job like `projects-sync`, so no new timers) |
| **meeting** | *presence* — join(url), a speaker-labelled transcript stream, say(text→voice), leave, who-joined/who-left | `meet` (browser path), `teams` (browser live + Graph post-meeting), `zoom` (RTMS receive; native send later) |
| **voice** | *ears and mouth* — stt(pcm) → text, tts(text, voiceId) → pcm | MLX sidecar (VibeVoice voices, Whisper/Parakeet/VibeVoice-ASR); a cloud STT/TTS is just another adapter |
| **channel** *(built)* | *conversation* — a meeting is a conversation without threads | the email channel (below) and the meeting conversation adapter |

The payoff is that a **meeting is a channel conversation**: a meeting
adapter feeding a transcript emits what a channel module emits — origin
(`meet`/`teams`/`zoom`), a channel id (the meeting), speaker-attributed
user lines. The ChannelEngine, link/identity, dreaming (meetings become
`facts.md` material!) and every command ride along for free. "aivi knew
the meeting happened and what was decided" needs no new memory machinery.

## Email — the other half, and it's small

An email channel is one more channel module on the built contract:
**`imapflow`** (IMAP with **IDLE** — server pushes, no polling, which
keeps the no-timers rule), **`mailparser`** for MIME, **`nodemailer`**
for SMTP. The open-source EmailEngine is built on imapflow, so the
pattern is proven at scale. A mailbox is the "conversation without
threads" case the Signal/Telegram research already flagged. This is the
cheapest new channel in the whole program and a good first build.

## Build order that makes sense

1. **Email channel** — `imapflow`/`nodemailer`, a channel module. Days,
   and it proves the role seams with zero media work.
2. **Calendar role + Google/Graph/CalDAV adapters** — watch channels +
   delta links; a `calendar.watch` system job; "join my 3pm" resolves a
   URL. No media yet: the calendar tells aivi *about* meetings.
3. **Meet browser adapter, listen-only** — Playwright `connectOverCDP`
   into the persistent Chrome, captions out, transcript into a channel
   conversation (dreaming gets meeting memory). The wow demo already:
   "what did we decide yesterday?"
4. **Zoom adapter on RTMS** — official Node SDK, webhooks in; proves the
   meeting role on a sanctioned platform.
5. **Voice out** — VibeVoice sidecar + fake-mic pump (BlackHole on the
   Mac host) into Meet; barge-in; the persisted-voice identity files in
   the home.
6. **Face** — canvas avatar as the video track. An afternoon of fun on
   top of 5.

## The fine print that matters

- **Consent is a feature, not a footnote.** Bots trigger recording-consent
  dialogs on Zoom; several jurisdictions require notice. aivi should
  announce itself in the meeting chat on join — which the identity work
  (`_worker: aivi_` signatures) already makes natural.
- **Echo loops are the known killer** (Zoom forum: translated-audio bot
  re-heard through participants' mics self-sustains). Barge-in plus
  mute-while-others-speak plus never playing aivi's own voice back into
  STT. The meeting-assistant reference handles exactly this.
- **ToS honesty**: Meet has no sanctioned bot path; the browser join is
  exactly what every commercial notetaker does, but it is automation of a
  Google account. Zoom (RTMS) and Teams (Graph) are the authorized doors.
  Worth a decision before shipping this to other people's installs.
- **Google Meet bot-detection** is real (puppeteer-extra issue #334);
  attaching to the user's own real Chrome over CDP is the mitigation the
  field converged on — and it is already aivi's shape.
